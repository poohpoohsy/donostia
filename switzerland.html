Four things before we go

  • The Jungfrau Travel Pass covers almost everything — trains, gondolas, cable cars, the postbus. When in doubt, just show it. The two exceptions are below.
  • Jungfraujoch costs extra: CHF 79 supplement plus CHF 10 seat reservation. Already booked for the 08:13 train on Day 4.
  • Download your tickets before you fly. Signal on the mountains is unreliable and you'll want them in your phone wallet, not in your email.
  • A few departures are fixed and won't wait — they're marked in amber on each day. Everything else is flexible.

Joining us today?

Two of us come in from Zürich and land at Grindelwald around 12:30 — roughly two hours to Interlaken Ost on the IC81, change, then thirty-five minutes up the valley.

Your afternoon: drop the checked bags at the hotel, then find us for lunch. Ninety minutes before the gondola, so there's no rush — but don't linger over coffee either.

Have boots and a warm layer where you can reach them. You'll go from a train to a 2,343m ridge inside two hours, and there's nowhere to repack in between.

If a connection slips, don't panic. The gondola runs continuously — take the next one and catch us on the trail, which is flat and easy walking. The only time that genuinely matters is being on the ridge by half three.

If the weather turns

Clear and bright: Schilthorn and the Bird Thrill Walk is the stronger day, if construction allows.

Low cloud: Allmendhubel alpine loop or a slow walk round Mürren village instead. No point paying for a view of the inside of a cloud.

08:41 → 11:15 · about 1.5 hrs up there

Jungfraujoch · 3,454m

It's all indoors and connected by tunnels and lifts — you never go outside except at the two viewpoints. Follow this order and we'll be ahead of the Interlaken crowds, who arrive mid-morning and queue for the Sphinx lift.

  1. OFF THE TRAIN
    Follow signs for the Jungfrau Panorama — a short 360° film, a few minutes, and a good place to let your body settle before the lifts.
  2. LIFT UP
    Sphinx Hall, then a second lift to the Sphinx Terrace, higher again. Outdoors, fenced, and the big one: Aletsch Glacier on one side, half of Europe on the other. There's a short snowy slope up to the platform, so watch your footing.
  3. BACK DOWN, THEN OUT
    Glacier Plateau — about ten minutes from the station and the only place you actually stand on the ice. Do it while the weather is holding; it closes in faster than the trains change.
  4. 250M CORRIDOR
    Alpine Sensation is the tunnel that connects Sphinx Hall to the Ice Palace — you walk through it rather than to it. Memorial plaques to the miners who cut the railway, and a giant snow globe at the end.
  5. AT THE END OF IT
    Ice Palace — tunnels cut inside the glacier, sculptures in the alcoves. Genuinely slippery. Shoes with grip, and hold the rail.
  6. BACK THE WAY YOU CAME
    The corridor is a there-and-back, so you'll re-walk it to reach the station. Allow ten minutes to get to the platform.

Altitude is real up here. Walk slowly, drink water, don't run up the stairs. If you feel light-headed or headachy, sit down and say so — it passes, but it's better managed early.

Optional afternoon — Glacier Gorge

A canyon cut by the glacier, with a net strung out over the meltwater that you can walk onto. About an hour inside — you don't need to go all the way to the far end, the net is the point.

Getting there: bus 122 from the village, ten minutes to the last stop, which is the entrance. Covered by the pass, just show it.

Getting back: 30 minutes downhill on foot, or the last bus 122 at 18:30. Suggestion — bus up, walk down. No timing risk and the valley is in front of you the whole way.

CHF 20 each to get in. The pass covers the bus, not the entrance — the one thing this week you pay for at the door.

Cold and damp inside even when the village is warm. Bring the jacket.

A few suggestions

Say something early, not late

There's a walk on Day 2 and two on Day 3. Every one of them has a bail-out — a station halfway, a train instead of a trail. Nobody has to finish anything. The only thing that spoils it is someone quietly struggling for an hour before mentioning it.

Boots, and one warm layer you actually trust

Three of the four days have a hike. Trainers will do on the flat, but the Panorama Trail and Bachalpsee are proper mountain paths and October ground can be wet.

Layers beat one thick coat. You'll be warm walking at 2,000m and cold standing still at 3,454m.

Sunglasses and sunscreen — seriously

Glare off snow and ice at altitude is far stronger than it looks, and October sun sits low. The Glacier Plateau on Day 4 is open ice.

Cash for the small stuff

Cards work almost everywhere, but mountain huts and small kiosks can be awkward. A little cash saves a queue.

Water bottle

Swiss tap water is excellent and there are fountains everywhere. Buying bottled water at 2,000m is its own kind of expensive.

Don't over-photograph the first viewpoint

The instinct on Day 3 is to spend twenty minutes at the Cliff Walk. The better view is an hour later at the lake, and the best is on the ridge that afternoon. Pace it.

Plans shift with the weather — Day 4 especially. If something changes I'll say so the night before, never on the morning.
