Entry

Passports — no visa needed

Good news

HKSAR passport holders don't need a visa. Switzerland, France and Spain are all Schengen — visa-free for 90 days in any 180. Our trip is sixteen.

No ETIAS either. The EU dropped its late-2026 target and hasn't set a new date. Nothing to apply for. If a website offers to sell you one, it's a scam.

But check two things

1 · Validity. Must be valid at least three months beyond 16 October 2026 — so mid-January 2027 minimum. Aim for more.

2 · Issue date. Must have been issued within the last ten years. This is the one that catches people. A passport issued in 2015 or earlier can be refused at the border even if the expiry date looks fine.

If either is a problem, start the renewal today.

1 October

Slow immigration at Zurich

The EU's Entry/Exit System replaced passport stamping and has been running since April. First entry means fingerprints and a facial scan, which takes longer than a stamp.

What this means for us

Queues of two to five hours were reported at some airports during the rollout. We land at 07:10 with a train to catch.

Expect it to be slow, don't panic, and we wait for each other before heading to the platform.

Electronics

Two different plugs — this catches people

Switzerland = Type J. Three round pins, unique to Switzerland.
France & Spain = Type C / E / F. Standard European two-pin.

A standard EU adapter will not fit older Swiss sockets. Bring a universal one, or one of each.

Both European flights

The baggage rule

Pack to this and you're safe on both

1 checked bag up to 23kg + 1 personal item only — a backpack or handbag that fits under the seat.

9 Oct, Geneva → Bilbao (easyJet): two checked bags of 23kg allowed, but no cabin bag at all — only the under-seat item.

15 Oct, Bilbao → Zurich (Swiss/Edelweiss): one checked bag of 23kg, plus a cabin bag up to 8kg.

So: no wheeled cabin case. easyJet won't take one on the 9th. If it doesn't fit under the seat in front of you, it goes in the hold.

You can check a second bag on the 9th if you need it — but not on the 15th, so anything extra has to be consolidated before we leave Spain.

Practical

Money

Switzerland uses Swiss Francs, not Euros. France and Spain use Euros. Some Swiss places take EUR but at a terrible rate.

Cards work almost everywhere, but bring cash — a few mountain restaurants and small pintxos bars are cash-only or card-shy.

Switzerland is genuinely expensive. Budget roughly CHF 25–35 for a simple mountain lunch.

Be honest with yourself

The pace

Most days start early. We leave the chalet at 07:45 on Day 2, check out at 07:00 on Day 3, and are at the Terminal by 07:55 on Day 4.

Then it flips completely in Spain, where dinner doesn't start until 20:30 and pintxos bars run late.

Get sleep on the flight over.

Altitude

Jungfraujoch at 3,454m and Aiguille du Midi at 3,842m are high enough that you may feel light-headed, breathless, or get a mild headache. It's normal.

Walk slowly, don't rush the stairs, drink water, and tell someone if you feel unwell. There's a "hypoxia level" exhibit at Aiguille du Midi — that's how thin the air is.

How pintxos works

Pintxos bars are standing-room, loud, and you order at the counter. The routine is one or two bites and one drink per bar, then move on.

Don't fill up at the first place. Cash is handy for the small ones.
