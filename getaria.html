Why they call it the mouse

Monte San Antón sits on a spit of land off the town, and from a distance the shape reads as a mouse with its tail running back to shore. Everything on this page is within fifteen minutes' walk of everything else. The dashed line is a twenty-minute climb to the lookout.

11:15 1 HR ROUND TRIP

Up the mouse — San Antón lookout

Monte San Antón · free · 4.6

Signposted clearly from the port. Twenty minutes up through thick vegetation on a made path — reviewers describe it as easy and suitable for anyone. At the top, an old watchtower site once used to spot storms, enemy ships and shoals of fish.

The lighthouse itself is closed — you can photograph it, not enter. Views over the Cantabrian in every direction.

13:00 → 15:00 BOOKED

Kaia Kaipe

General Arnao Kalea 4 · 4.4 from 2,236 · top price bracket

Order thisThe grilled turbot — it's what people come for. Plus txangurro, the spider crab stew, and langoustines. Three kinds of bread and the olive oil get mentioned as often as the fish.

Overlooking the port. Old-school, calm, formal-ish service. Local Getaria txakoli is the obvious wine and it's made on the hillside above the town.

Worth knowing: not every review is a rave. One regular thinks it's overpriced for what it is and says you can eat similar fish down the street for a fraction. It's the most expensive meal of the trip after Akelarre — go in expecting an occasion, not a bargain.

15:15 → 16:30 FREE

Mooch

Back down to the harbour. Fish drying on racks, boats coming in, and the txakoli shops along the main street — buy it here, not at the airport. It's made on the hill you can see above the town.

If anyone wants sand, there's a small sheltered beach on the town side of the isthmus.

18:30 → 20:00 SUNSET ~19:40

Urgulleko Polboriña

Monte Urgull · bar in the old gunpowder magazine · 4.7 from 918 · cheap

The moveClimb Urgull from the old town, drink on the terrace, watch the sun go down over the bay.

A tiny bar hidden inside the fortifications halfway up the hill. Reviewers call it a hidden gem and warn you'll miss it if you don't look for the small bar sign and arrow. Open daily 11:00–20:45, so sunset at 19:40 sits perfectly inside.

Get there by 18:45. One review puts it plainly: it's a great sunset spot but hard to get a seat. Thirty minutes up from the old town, so leave around 18:15.

Photo: the terrace looks straight down over the bay and the Christ statue stands above you at the summit. Go up to the castle first for the 360°, then drop back to the bar for the light.

Alternative IF LEGS HAVE GONE

La Concha promenade

Kontxa Pasealekua · 4.8 from 2,951 · flat, free, always open

No climb. The bay faces west, so the sun goes down across the water in front of you. One reviewer: catching this place at golden hour is really something.

Photo: the white balustrade railings are the signature of the city — shoot along them with the bay behind. Isla Santa Clara sits in the middle of the frame.

Drink: Café de La Concha sits right on the promenade, open till 23:00. Only 3.8 and pricier than town — you're paying for the terrace, not the coffee.

The bits you'll remember

Five moments, not a shot list

Nobody wants a day of being posed. These are just the ones worth not missing — mostly because they only happen here.

  • The smoke on Kale Nagusia. Fish grills sit outside the restaurant doors on the main street, whole turbot over coals. The smell hits before you see it. Walk down slowly and film the street, not the fish.
  • The txakoli pour. It's poured from arm's length above the glass to break the fizz. It looks like showing off and it isn't. Someone film it — nobody at home believes it otherwise.
  • Six at the lookout. The one group photo of the day.
    What you'll see: a bare grassy headland with a stubby old watchtower and a lighthouse you can photograph but not enter. Open sea on three sides, the town and its beach below on the fourth. Reviewers describe it as an easy signposted climb through thick green vegetation, twenty minutes from the port.

  • Looking back at the mouse. From the coast path toward Zarautz, turn round.
    What you'll see: the headland in profile across the water — a rounded body rising to a snout at the seaward end, with the low isthmus running back to town as the tail. From inside Getaria it just looks like a hill. From here it doesn't, and that's the whole joke of the name.

  • Bread and oil before the fish. Three kinds of bread arrive first at Kaia Kaipe, with a small mushroom soup sent out on the house. Reviewers mention them as often as the turbot.

One rule for the day

No photos at the table for the first ten minutes. It's a two-hour lunch at the best fish restaurant on this coast, we're six friends, and the food will still be there. Eat first.

Practical

Worth knowing

  • Shoes you can climb in. The San Antón path is easy but it's a real path, and the old town street is steep and cobbled.
  • A layer. The lookout is exposed on all sides and it's the Cantabrian in October.
  • Don't eat breakfast heavily. Lunch is the day.
  • Cash for the bus. Worth having coins rather than relying on a card.
  • Buy txakoli here, not at the airport. It's made on the hill above the town and the price reflects that.
