First cable car · 08:10

Be at the station by 07:45. Nothing runs before 08:10 in October — and sunrise here is around 07:45, so there's no point arriving earlier.

Still be on the first car. Cloud builds through the morning, and the earliest passengers get the mountain everyone else photographs from below.

At the top · about two hours

Do it in this order

The layout is a maze of terraces, lifts and tunnels across two summits joined by a bridge. Follow this and you won't double back.

  1. Lower terrace first — but don't cross the bridge yet. Climb the staircases to the upper terrace instead, points 16–17 on the site map. That's the best viewpoint on the mountain and almost everyone walks straight past it.
  2. Now the bridge, across to the central peak.
  3. Espace Vertical — the climbing and alpinism gallery.
  4. Terrace 3842, up by lift. This is the Void — a glass box cantilevered over a thousand-metre drop. Shoe covers provided. Not optional.
  5. Espace Mont Blanc.
  6. The Hypoxia level — on what thin air does to a body.
  7. Le Tube, the walkway wrapping the outside of the summit.
  8. The open-air terrace, point 12. Stand still and notice the breathing. That's the point of it.
  9. Gondola to Pointe Helbronner, Italy — closed for the season since 27 September.

Opens 08:10 · last departure for a round trip 15:00 · last one-way 16:00.

Reservations are free but mandatory. New tickets are released about three days before the date, and same-day box-office tickets depend on availability. Book, and if the early slots look gone, check again at three days out.

Round trip runs €60.20–83.00 per adult — dynamic pricing, so earlier booking is cheaper.

Step into the Void isn't guaranteed

The operator states access can be withdrawn for overcrowding, lift unavailability, very low temperatures, heavy frost or strong winds. At 3,842m in October several of those are live possibilities. Worth wanting rather than expecting.

3,842m is high

Nearly 400m higher than Jungfraujoch, and you get there in twenty minutes rather than acclimatising. Move slowly, drink water, don't take the stairs at pace. Light-headedness and a dull headache are common and pass — say so early rather than pushing through.

Also genuinely cold and windy on the open terraces. Gloves.

The weather rule

This is the day worth watching. In cloud you pay a lot to stand inside one. If Tuesday looks poor and Wednesday looks better, we swap Days 6 and 7 — the Balcon walk is fine under cloud, the summit isn't.

09:05 → 13:05 · 7 km, four hours

The traverse

Little net height gain, but the ground is rough and there are short scrambling sections. Reviewers who quote 2.5 hours were moving fast alone; a mixed group takes four. That's what we've planned for.

There's a fork partway. Higher is harder with a better view, lower is easier. We're taking the lower line — decided, so nobody's negotiating at a junction.

Bossons glacier behind you for the first half, Mer de Glace ahead for the second.

13:20

Packed lunch on the terrace

Bought last night. The Montenvers terrace looks straight down the Mer de Glace, and eating our own means no dependence on what's open up here in October.

Everyone carries their own lunch, snacks and water. There's no refill point on the balcony and nothing to buy until we arrive.

14:30

Glaciorium, then the terrace

A small museum on how glaciers form and how this one has retreated — about fifteen minutes, five minutes' walk from the station. Then the Temple de la Nature and the old railway hotel above the ice.

No ice cave and no gondola down to the glacier — both closed for the season. The view from above is the day's ending.

The Glaciorium and Temple de la Nature should be open — only the gondola and cave are listed as shut. Worth one call to confirm: 04 65 60 00 80.

At the top

The view back

This is the frame every photograph of Chamonix is taken from. There's a restaurant at the summit and a terrace, and after two hard days it's fine to make this a sitting-down day.

If legs allow — the Grand Balcon Sud

A ridge path from Planpraz running along the Aiguilles Rouges side with Mont Blanc opposite the whole way. Note the Flégère lift at the far end is shut for the season, so it's out and back rather than a crossing — turn round whenever you like.

Then stop

Back down mid-afternoon. Rest, shower, pack properly and eat early. The alarm goes at half one.
